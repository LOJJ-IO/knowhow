Information architecture

The current routes grew one feature at a time. This is the structure the same data layer supports without a single query changing — src/lib/queries.ts already returns everything below.

Today

6 flat routes
/dashboard role-branched
Renders two different products at one URL. The owner has no personal workspace; members have no org view.
/org-chart owner + teams
A two-step form, not a chart — no hierarchy is ever drawn.
/team/[id]/people roster
Clicking a team in the sidebar lands on staffing, not that team's documents — the opposite of what the dashboard implies.
/activity audit trail
/settings sharing policies
— documents
No home. Documents exist only as boards embedded in dashboards, and search is a dropdown with nowhere to land.
— visibility
No surface. Coverage is computable but never shown, so the red dots stay decorative.

Proposed

6 sections, role-aware O Owner L Leader M Member
Home OLM

One route, two compositions: the owner gets an org pulse, everyone else gets their workspace. Today these are the same URL rendering two different products.

Documents OLM

Documents finally get a home of their own, with a real results page behind the search field.

All documents OL
Shared with me OLM
My documents OLM
Drive folders OL

The auto-filing map — already computed by getFolderRouting().

Visibility 15 OL

Promoted to top level: the gap the product exists to close becomes a queue you can act on, not a red dot you can only look at.

Organization O
Org chart O
Team hub — Documents · People · Policy OL

Three disconnected screens today; one hub with tabs here.

Activity OLM

Audit trail, filterable by event type.

Settings O

Org profile, Workspace connection, and the per-team sharing policies.

What this costs

Nothing in src/lib, prisma/ or the server actions changes. Two new routes (/documents, /visibility), one route merged (/team/[id] gains tabs), and the sidebar becomes role-aware. The onboarding, offboarding and auto-share engines keep their verified behaviour.
